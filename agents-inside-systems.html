05 · Agentic automation

Agentic automation inside the organisation's core systems

COAR Global, 2025 to 2026. Four agents in production; a fifth specified.

A recruitment analysis inside the ERP, a report-format check for the designer, a team report that replaced a recurring meeting, and a morning trend brief in Slack. In each one the model reads and explains, code holds the rules and the decision stays with the person. A fifth agent, on stale items in the ERP, is proposed.

At a glance

Users
Recruiters in the ERP, the graphic designer, the country teams, analysts in Slack.
Problem
Routine reading that follows the same steps every time: an application against a job description, a report against its format and palette, team updates spread over task lists, boards and spreadsheets, the morning's news against the organisation's own research.
Hypothesis
Put an agent inside the system the person already uses. Let the model read and explain, keep scheduling and thresholds in code, and leave the decision with a person, on a record that shows why.
What shipped
Four agents in production, each with its output where the person already works. A fifth, on stale ERP items, is proposed and in development.
Decision owner
The recruiter shortlists or refuses; the designer signs off; the team follows up on its report; the analyst chooses what to pursue. Each new AI use is decided in writing by the AI Policy and Practice Group.
Success measure
Outputs checked against the manual result, with review starting at 100 percent; one meeting removed; no decision taken by a model; every refusal and sign-off still a human action.

Five agents, side by side

1. Recruitment analysis, inside the ERP

Every new application arrives as attachments and fields: a CV in PDF or Word, a cover letter, a figure for years of experience. Each one is read against the job description, the same reading for every applicant. Version 1 (2025, written by a developer) had the model summarise and a fixed rule score. Version 2, rebuilt in November 2026 with Claude Code, drops the scores: the model gives its reading in words, on the first tab of the record, and the recruiter decides.

Live since November 2026. A verdict is one of four words (strong, possible, weak, not a fit) with the reasons beside it; there is no number to sort by, so the recruiter reads before deciding. A button reruns the analysis on demand.

  • Who decides. The recruiter: refusing or shortlisting a candidate is a human action in the ERP.
  • Added since. Grouping and filtering applicants by country experience, and a nationality field, in August and September 2026; version 2 in November 2026.
  • Data. CVs and applicant data are handled inside the ERP, under the AI use policy of June 2026 and its Green, Amber and Red classes.

4. Morning trend brief

News arrives through RSS feeds, unmerged and with no link to the organisation's own research. The brief joins the two at 08:00 Cyprus time: the day's items, grounded in the research corpus.

In production, 2026. Grounding goes through the knowledge platform's retrieval API. The workflow runs on the self-hosted automation platform (n8n), which holds six production workflows, each pulled to JSON and version-controlled. In September 2026 the AI Policy and Practice Group decided to keep it self-hosted, with no external subscription.

  • Where it lands. The Slack channel. The sources are exported on each run. No new tool to open.
  • Who decides. The analyst, who chooses what to follow up.

Design rules behind all five

The model proposes, a person decides, and the record shows why.

  • Outputs where people work. The applicant record, the designer's report, the team's inbox, the Slack channel. No new tool to open.
  • The model explains, code schedules. The model reads and explains; scheduling, thresholds and the record itself stay in code.
  • Validations in the system. Rules are kept hard to break by the system itself, rather than reminded to people.
  • Review starts at 100 percent. A new automation has every output reviewed against the manual result, and the share steps down as measured accuracy holds. No review share is stated for these four agents.
  • The group decides new uses. The AI Policy and Practice Group decides each new AI use every two weeks, in writing.

What was learned

  • A reading in words beats a score. Version 1 scored applicants with a fixed rule and recruiters sorted by the number. Version 2 gives a verdict with its reasons and no number; the recruiter reads the case and still takes every refusal and shortlist.
  • The meeting could go because the sources already existed. Every member's work was already in task lists, kanban boards and spreadsheets. The workflow reads what is already there.
  • One pattern repeats: extract and structure. A CV into strengths and gaps, a report into a list of faults, task boards into one report, news feeds into a brief.
