02 · AI workflow in production

AI screening of funding opportunities across seven portals

COAR Global business development. Live since August 2026.

Seven public portals polled three times a day and the alert mailbox read every fifteen minutes, every notice categorised and scored against the organisation's criteria, hot leads alerted at once, every discard logged with its reason, an analyst making each call. The first QA on live data produced a fix; the owner's first review produced the next change set.

At a glance

Users
The business development lead and two colleagues; an analyst makes each go or no-go call.
Problem
Opportunities spread across many portals and alert mails; manual search found the relevant ones late or not at all, and nothing recorded why a notice was passed over.
Hypothesis
Collect everything, let a model categorise and explain, keep eligibility and thresholds in code, and log every discard so the screen can be audited.
What shipped
Two intakes, seven pollers, one screening chain, a tracker sheet, alerts within the run, an 08:00 digest, a discard register, an analytics sheet, an evaluation set.
Decision owner
The business development lead owns the tracker since the walkthrough of 27 August 2026; the AI Policy and Practice Group hears each change.
Success measure
Categorisation accuracy against the evaluation set, relevant notices surfaced that manual search missed, time the team no longer spends searching.

The workflow runs on a self-hosted automation platform (n8n), pulled to JSON and version-controlled; the pollers are code in the same repository.

  1. Collect. Seven pollers run at 07:00, 12:00 and 17:00 Cyprus time. The alert mailbox is read every 15 minutes.
  2. Normalise. Every notice, from a portal or from a mail, is mapped to one schema.
  3. Pre-filter. A gate in code on the country and on the nature of the procurement.
  4. De-duplicate. Against both sheets, by reference and by title plus deadline.
  5. Categorise and score. A model categorises the notice against the organisation's capabilities and scores the probability of winning.
  6. Route. A notice at or above the 60 percent threshold that a private company may bid for goes to the tracker sheet and is emailed to the leads within the run. Everything else goes to the discard register with a reason.
  7. Digest. A separate schedule at 08:00 sends the daily digest.
  8. Record. A third sheet holds analytics.
  9. Decide. An analyst makes each go or no-go call.

Seven portals and one mailbox

First week on live data

What the first QA found

  • When. 18 August 2026, the first QA on live data, one day after the pollers went live.
  • Found. No grant had ever reached the scorer.
  • Cause. The Grants.gov branch labelled every notice "Grant". The pre-filter's nature gate passed only consulting, service, research, study, technical assistance, advisory, analysis, evaluation and monitoring.
  • Fix. The gate was widened to accept grant, cooperative agreement and funding opportunity.

Owner review, six weeks after the handover

Scoring rule, before and after

Cost and evaluation

  • Cost. About USD 7 a month, on the organisation's own infrastructure, with no subscriptions.
  • Evaluation set. It checks the model's categorisation against known-correct answers.

Human in the loop, by design

An analyst makes every go or no-go call. Nothing is deleted. Ownership of the tracker was handed to the business development lead with a walkthrough; her comments on the sheet are the change channel, and each change is reported to the AI Policy and Practice Group.

What was learned

  • Model for judgement, code for rules. Categorisation and explanation belong to the model; eligibility, thresholds and bands belong in code where they are deterministic and auditable.
  • Log every discard. The register made the owner's review possible and turned her findings into a change set in one sitting.
  • Hand ownership to the person whose work it replaces. The tracker's owner found what the builder could not see.
  • QA each branch on live data. Every poller returned data on every run, and one branch still sent nothing to the scorer.

Role

Gerard Bou Faical, Head of Technology and AI Consultant, COAR Global and member of the senior management team, designed and built the workflow, the portal pollers and the evaluation set, operates it, handed ownership to the business development lead and reports each change to the AI Policy and Practice Group.
